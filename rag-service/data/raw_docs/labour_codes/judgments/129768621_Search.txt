We have heard the Counsel for the parties, and, have gone through the evidence and record of the case, carefully.
The first question, that falls for consideration, is as to whether, this Commission has got territorial Jurisdiction to entertain and decide the consumer complaint, or not. No doubt, the Counsel for Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, submitted that since the parties as per Clause 36 of the Buyer Agreement dated 12.04.2014 Annexure C-1, agreed that the Courts at Delhi alone, would have Jurisdiction, for adjudication of all matters arising out or in connection with the same (Agreement), this Commission has got no territorial Jurisdiction, to entertain and decide the complaint. The submission of the Counsel for Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, in this regard, being devoid of merit, is liable to be rejected, for the reasons, to be recorded hereinafter. In the first instance, it may be stated here that the Consumer Foras' are not the Courts. Clause 36 of the Buyer Agreement dated 12.04.2014 Annexure C-1, only confers Jurisdiction on the Courts at Delhi. Under these circumstances, no help can be drawn, from this Clause for coming to the conclusion that this Commission at Chandigarh, has no territorial Jurisdiction, to entertain and decide the instant complaint.
In
Associated Road Carriers Ltd. Vs. Kamlender Kashyap and Ors., I
(2008) CPJ 404 (NC), it was held by the National Commission, that a clause of Jurisdiction, by way of an agreement, between the parties, could not be made applicable, to the Consumer Complaints, filed before the Consumer Foras, as the Foras are not the Courts.  It was further held, in the said case, that there is a difference between
Section 11
of the Act, which a para materia to
Section 17
of the Act and the provisions  of
Sections 15
to
20
of the Civil Procedure Code, regarding the place of Jurisdiction. In the instant case, the Buyer Agreement dated 12.04.2014 was made at Chandigarh. The letters dated 27.09.2011 Annexure C-2, and 05.05.2014 Annexure C-4, clearly establish that the same were issued by the Chandigarh Office of the Opposite Parties, to the complainant, in respect of confirmation of payments made, towards the unit, in question. As such, a part of cause of action, accrued to the complainant, at Chandigarh. Accordingly, this Commission at  Chandigarh, in view of the provisions of
Section 17
of the Act, has territorial Jurisdiction to entertain and decide the complaint. The submission of the Counsel for Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, in this regard, therefore being devoid of merit, must fail and the same stands rejected.
No doubt, the Counsel for Opposite Parties No.1,2,3 and 5, placed reliance on  M/s Swastik Gases P. Ltd. Versus Indian Oil Corporation Ltd., Civil Appeal No. 5086 of  2013,  arising out of SLP (Civil) No.5595 of 2012,  decided on 03.07.2013, by the Hon'ble Supreme Court of India, to contend that where the agreement/ contract specifies the Jurisdiction of the Court at a particular place, all other Courts are excluded from invoking the same (Jurisdiction). It may be stated here, that, as stated above, the Consumer Foras' are not the Courts. There is a difference between
Section 11
of the Act, which a para materia to
Section 17
of the Act and the provisions  of
Sections 15
to
20
of the Civil Procedure Code, regarding the place of Jurisdiction In
Ethiopian Airlines Vs Ganesh Narain Saboo, IV
(2011) CPJ 43 (SC) = VII  (2011) SLT 371, the principle  of law, laid down, was to the effect, that the restriction of Jurisdiction to a particular Court, need not be given any importance in the  circumstances of the case.
In
Cosmos Infra Engineering India Ltd. Vs Sameer Saksena & another
I (2013) CPJ 31 (NC) and  Radiant Infosystem Pvt. Ltd. & Others Vs D.Adhilakshmi & Anr I  (2013) CPJ 169 (NC), the agreements were executed, between the Parties, incorporating therein, a condition, excluding the Jurisdiction of any other Court/Forum, in case of dispute, arising under the same, and limiting the Jurisdiction to the Courts/Forums at Delhi, and Hyderabad. The National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission, New Delhi, in the aforesaid cases, held that such a condition, incorporated in the agreements, executed between the Parties, excluding the Jurisdiction of a particular Court/Forum, and limiting the Jurisdiction to a particular Court/Forum, could not be given any importance, and the complaint could be filed, at a place, where a part of cause of action arose, according to
Sections 11
/
17
of the Act. Under these circumstances Clause 36 of the Buyer Agreement Annexure C-1, being in breach of
Section 17
of the Act, could not operate.
No help, therefore, can be drawn, by the Counsel for Opposite Parties No.1,2,3, and 5, from  M/s
Swastik Gases P. Ltd.
's case (supra). The submission of the Counsel for Opposite Parties No.1,2,3, and 5, in this regard, therefore, being devoid of merit, must fail, and the same stands rejected.
It was next submitted by the Counsel for Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, that this Commission has no pecuniary Jurisdiction to entertain and decide the complaint. He further submitted that possession of the flat/unit, in question, was offered to the complainant, vide demand letter/legal notice dated 20.08.2014 Annexure C-6, on payment of amount of Rs.5,68,857.53Ps., being due against him. He further submitted that the complainant was given time of 30 days, to deposit the said amount, failing which, it was made clear that the allotment of unit No.6702,  in his favour shall be cancelled. He further submitted that since the possession of unit No.6702 was ready and the same was offered to the complainant, vide the letter aforesaid, by seeking the possession in the complaint, he only wanted to usurp the pecuniary Jurisdiction of this Commission. He further submitted that if the amount of reliefs, is clubbed together, then only the District Forum had the pecuniary Jurisdiction to entertain and decide the complaint. The submission of the Counsel for Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, in this regard, is devoid of merit, and is liable to be rejected. It is, no doubt, true that vide demand letter/legal notice dated 20.08.2014 Annexure C-6, an intimation was given to the complainant, to the effect, that the unit was ready for possession, and the payment of amount due again him be made. However, the demand raised in the said letter was challenged by the complainant, being illegal. There was no clear-cut offer of possession, vide demand letter/legal notice dated 20.08.2014 Annexure C-6. On the other hand, it was only an intimation that the flat/unit was ready for possession. This letter carried a rider that the remaining amount was allegedly due against the complainant. Since the complainant considered the demand raised by Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, to be illegal, there was no alternative with him than to challenge the same, and seek possession by way of filing the consumer complaint. Had the possession been actually offered to the complainant without any rider, the matter would have been different. Since possession of the unit has also been sought, in the complaint, the value of the same is also required to be added to the amount of other reliefs, so as to determine the pecuniary Jurisdiction of this Commission. Once the price of the unit to the tune of Rs.30,86,800/- and amount of other reliefs are clubbed, only one and one conclusion that can be arrived at, is that this Commission has pecuniary Jurisdiction to entertain and decide the complaint. The submission of the Counsel for Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, in this regard, therefore being devoid of merit, must fail and the same stands rejected.
It was next submitted by the Counsel for Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, that the consumer complaint was not filed by a duly authorized Power of Attorney of the complainant, and, as such, the same is liable to be dismissed. He further submitted that vide Special Power of Attorney, copy whereof is Annexure C-11, the complainant only authorized Mr.Tajinder Singh Bajwa, his Attorney to pursue any required action, in respect of the unit, in question, in the lower Courts, High Court and the Supreme Court of India. He further submitted that since the attorney was not authorized to file the complaint, in the Consumer Fora, he was not competent to do so. The submission of the Counsel for Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, does not appear to be correct. It may be stated here, that the
Consumer Protection Act, 1986
, is a beneficial legislation meant to provide speedy inexpensive and hassle free redressal to the grievance of the consumers. The provisions
of the Code of Civil Procedure
, except the one contained in
Section 13 (4)
of the Act, are not applicable to the consumer disputes. The Consumer Foras are to evolve their own procedure, for adjudicating the consumer disputes, by resorting to the principles of natural justice. Perusal of Clause I of the Special Power of Attorney Annexure C-11, establishes that the Attorney was also authorized to take legal action against the builder M/s Premium Acres Infratech Private Limited and M/s Taneja Developers and Infrastructure Limited to obtain legal completion of the property and title and ownership in the principal's name. Thus, Clause 1 of the Special Power of Attorney, Annexure C-11, duly authorized the Attorney namely Tajinder Singh Bajwa, to initiate any legal action, against the Opposite Parties, for the purpose of obtaining possession. Under these circumstances, by no stretch of imagination, it could be said that the consumer complaint was not filed by a duly authorized Power of Attorney of the complainant. The submission of the Counsel for Opposite Parties No.1,2,3 and 5, in this regard, therefore being devoid of merit, must fail and the same stands rejected.
The next question, that falls for consideration, is, as to whether, the complainant fell within the definition of a consumer, as defined by
Section 2 (1) (d) (ii)
of the Act, or not. The complainant, no doubt, is a Non-Resident Indian, (NRI), and is settled in England. It was the residential flat/unit, which was allotted, in favour of the complainant, in the manner
,
referred to above
. The mere objection of the Opposite Parties, that the complainant is a Non-Resident Indian (NRI), and is settled in England, though, originally belonged to India, and, thus he did not purchase a residential flat/unit, in India, with a view to have his residence, in the same, for the purpose of his permanent settlement, but, on the other hand, being speculator/investor, he purchased the same (apartment), for resale thereof, as and when there was escalation, in prices, and, as such, he did not fall within  the definition of a 'consumer', as per
Section 2(1)(d)(ii)
of the Act, does not carry any weight and the same deserves to be rejected. It was clearly averred by the complainant, in his rejoinder, that he purchased the residential flat/unit, in question, in the project of Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, for the purpose of spending his life peacefully, amongst his near and dear ones. Even otherwise, the mere fact that it was a residential flat/unit, which was purchased by the complainant, was sufficient to prove that it was to be used for the purpose of residence, by him, may be off and on, on his visit to India, or for the purpose of his residence. There is nothing, on the record, that the complainant is a property dealer, and deals in the sale and purchase of property. No evidence was also produced by the Opposite Parties, to prove that the complainant owned a number of other residential properties, in the tricity, and, as such, unit No.6702, was purchased by him, by way of investment, with a view to resell the same, as and when, there was escalation in the prices thereof, or to rent out the same. No law of the land, debars NRI, though originally belonging to India, to purchase a residential property, in India, with a view to reside therein. The complainant, thus, availed of the services of the Opposite Parties, for consideration, for the allotment of unit No.6702, with a view to reside in the same. The complainant, thus, fell within the definition of a consumer, as defined by
Section 2(1)(d)(ii)
of the Act. Such an objection, taken by Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, in their written statement, therefore, being devoid of merit, is rejected.
The next question, that falls for consideration, is, as to within which period, the delivery of possession of unit No.6702, was to be given to the complainant. According to Clause 9 of the Buyer Agreement dated 12.04.2014, Annexure C-1,  subject to force majeure conditions and reasons, beyond the control of Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, they were to deliver physical possession of unit No.6702 within a maximum period of 24 months, from the date of execution of the same (Agreement). It is, thus, evident, from this Clause, that the Opposite Parties, are required to deliver possession of unit No.6702 in favour of  the complainant, within 24 months, from the date of execution of the Buyer Agreement dated 12.04.2014, Annexure C-1 i.e. latest by 11.04.2016. The submission made by the Counsel for the complainant, in the absence of production of any other Agreement, except the one placed on record i.e. Buyer Agreement dated 12.04.2014, Annexure C-1, that possession of unit No.6702, was to be delivered by the Opposite Parties, by 01.07.2013, being devoid of merit, must fail, and the same stands rejected. The Opposite Parties could not be held deficient, in rendering service, on this score.
The next question, that falls for consideration, is, as to what was the actual price of unit No.6702. It may be stated here, that the complainant opted for the construction linked instalment payment plan.  Thus, as per the payment schedule attached with the Buyer Agreement dated 12.04.2014, Annexure C-1,  the basic sale price of unit No.6702, was Rs.27,00,000/- plus (+) Rs.1,16,800/- as External Development Charges plus (+) Rs.2,70,000/- as Preferential Location Charges i.e. total Rs.30,86,800/-. As such, the total price of unit No.6702, which the complainant was required to pay was Rs.30,86,800/-.  On the other hand, it is evident from the letter dated 05.05.2014 Annexure C-4, issued by Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, to the complainant, that "the payment of Rs.0 is pending as on date on account of 100% of completion alongwith late payment interest charges". Not only this, alongwith the said letter dated 05.05.2014 Annexure C-4, the statement of account was also attached. As per this statement of account, at page 49 of the file, the complainant had already paid an amount of Rs.35,03,275/-, which included the price of unit No.6702, as also the miscellaneous charges towards water, electricity, sewerage etc. etc. It means that the entire sale consideration, including other charges, had already been paid by the complainant by 05.05.2014. No doubt, demand letter/legal notice dated 20.08.2014 Annexure C-6, was sent by Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, to the complainant, claiming that payment of Rs.5,68,857.53Ps. was pending on account of service tax, club charges, Interest Free Maintenance Security, water, sewerage and electric charges, service tax on services,  delayed payment interest, inflation, additional maintenance charges etc. alongwith pending basic price and PLC. However, as stated above, as per letter dated 05.05.2014 Annexure C-4, as also the statement of account, attached therewith, signed by an authorized signatory of Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, the total amount received from the complainant, towards the price of unit No.6702, alongwith other charges was shown as Rs.35,03,275/-. The remaining outstanding against the complainant was shown as "Zero".  It means that according to these documents, nothing was due against the complainant to Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, as arrears towards unit No.6702, which was allotted in his favour, in the manner
,
referred to above
. Annexure C-4 letter dated 05.05.2014, accompanied by  the statement of account
,
referred to above
, contained the admission of Opposite Parties No.1,2,3 and 5, that nothing was due to them, against the complainant, in so far as the unit, in question, was concerned. Thus, the submission of the Counsel for the complainant, to the effect that the entire amount which was due towards the price of unit No.6702, plus (+) other charges had been paid by the complainant, to Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5 carries substance and stands accepted.
No doubt, the Counsel  for Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, submitted that letter dated 05.05.2014 Annexure C-4, as also the statement of account, attached therewith, were issued by Mr. Sanjay Jain, their Ex-Director, who was not authorized to do so. He further submitted that it was found that he (Sanjay Jain) had embezzled huge amount of the Company and had played fraud with it and, as such, a criminal case against him and the complainant was lodged. He further submitted that no reliance, therefore, on the documents
,
referred to above
, could be placed. The submission of the Counsel  for  Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, in this regard, does not appear to be correct. If there was any dispute between Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5 and its Director Sanjay Jain and he allegedly played fraud with the Company as also allegedly embezzled its amount, then the third parties i.e. the consumers were not to suffer. The consumers, while dealing with the Company, did not know as to who were the Directors thereof, and how their internal affairs were being managed. In case, Mr. Sanjay Jain, former Director of Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5,  allegedly played fraud with them, or allegedly embezzled the amount belonging to the Company, then the consumers could not be held liable to suffer for that. Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5 had their legal remedy of recovery of the amount allegedly embezzled by Sanjay Jain, Ex-Director, by filing a Civil Suit and also proceeding against him under the criminal law. Thus, letter dated 05.05.2014 Annexure C-4, as also the statement of account, attached therewith, could not be said to be unreliable documents. Once, in letter dated 05.05.2014 Annexure C-4, as also the statement of account, attached therewith, outstanding shown against the complainant was Zero, it is not known, as to how, Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, served legal notice dated 20.08.2014, demanding the amount of Rs.5,68,857.53Ps. Opposite Parties No.1,2 3 and 5 could not wriggle out the letter dated 05.05.2014 Annexure C-4, as also the statement of account, attached therewith, as the same contained admission against their interests. This demand was, therefore, illegal and arbitrary and liable to be set aside.  By raising the illegal demand, Opposite Parties No.1,2,3 and 5, indulged into unfair trade practice.
The next question, that falls for consideration, is, as to whether, the allotment of unit No.6702, was legally and validly cancelled by Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, vide letter dated 06.10.2014 Annexure C-10. It may be stated here, that once it is held that the demand raised by Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, vide demand letter/legal notice dated 20.08.2014 Annexure C-6, was illegal and arbitrary,  the cancellation made by them, vide letter dated 06.10.2014 Annexure C-10, could also be said to be illegal and arbitrary. By cancelling the allotment of unit No.6702, illegally and arbitrarily, Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5 were not only deficient, in rendering service, but also indulged into unfair trade practice, on this score.
The next question, that falls for consideration, is, as to whether, the complainant is entitled to compensation, under
Section 14 (1) (d)
of the Act, on account  of non-delivery of possession of unit No.6702, and for alleged mental agony and physical harassment caused to him. As  stated  above,  as   per    Clause 9   of   the   Buyer  Agreement dated 12.04.2014, Annexure C-1,  subject to force majeure conditions and reasons, beyond the control of Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, they were to deliver physical possession of unit No.6702, within a maximum period of 24 months, from the date of execution of the same (Agreement) i.e. latest by  11.04.2016. Since the allotment of unit was illegally cancelled, by Opposite Parties No.1,2,3 and 5, cause of action arose to the complainant to file the complaint. As such, by no stretch of imagination, it could be said that as on date, there was any delay, in delivery of possession of unit No.6702, to the complainant. Therefore, the question of causing mental agony and physical harassment to the complainant at the hands of Opposite Parties No.1,2,3 and 5, also did not at all arise. Thus, in our considered opinion, the complainant is not entitled to any compensation, on this score. The submission of the Counsel for the complainant, in this regard, being devoid of merit, must fail, and the same stands rejected.
Coming to the liability of Opposite Party No.4, it may be stated here, that the latter was not a party to the Buyer Agreement dated 12.04.2014, Annexure C-1. Even the services of Opposite Party No.4 were not hired by the complainant, and, as such, the question of payment of consideration to it, never arose. Hence, the complaint against Opposite Party No.4 is liable to be dismissed.
For the reasons recorded above, the complaint is partly accepted, with costs, against Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5. Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5 are jointly and severally held liable and directed as under:_


 
	 To hand over legal physical possession of unit No.6702, complete in all respects, within three months, from the date of receipt of a certified copy of this order.
To execute the sale deed and get the same registered, in respect of unit No.6702 in favour of the complainant, on payment of stamp duty and  registration charges by the latter (complainant), within a period of 1 (one) month, from the date of delivery of possession.
To pay Rs.20,000/- as cost of litigation.
Demand letter/Legal notice dated 20.08.2014 Annexure C-6, issued by Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, being illegal, null and void, is set aside.
Cancellation of allotment of unit No.6702, vide letter dated 06.10.2014 Annexure C-10, issued by Opposite Parties No.1, 2, 3 and 5, being illegal and arbitrary is also set aside.
Complaint against Opposite Party No.4, is dismissed with no order as to costs.
Certified Copies of this order be sent to the parties, free of charge.
