/

2.          The petition is premised on Memorandum of Understanding dated
                                                04.11.2024, whereby the petitioner and respondents Nos. 2 and 3 have
                                                resolved the matter amicably.
3.          The petition is also supported by affidavits of the petitioners and of
                                                respondents Nos. 2 and 3, alongwith proof of their IDs.
4.          The contesting parties are present in court. Their credentials have
                                                been verified and they have also been identified by the Investigating
                                                Officer.
5.          The court has interacted with respondent No. 3, who has confirmed
                                                that she has signed Memorandum of Understanding dated 04.11.2024
                                                and wishes to close the subject FIR against the petitioner.
6.          At the outset, Mr. Hitesh Vali, learned APP for the State objects to
                                                quashing of the subject FIR. Learned APP submits that vide order
                                                dated 12.11.2020 made by the learned Additional Sessions Judge, Tis
                                                Hazari Courts, Delhi, charges have been framed against the petitioner
                                                under
sections 363
/
366
/
376(2)(n)
/
174-A
IPC as well as under
section
                                                6
of the POCSO Act.
7.          Mr. Vali submits, that in view of the decision of the Supreme Court in
Ramji Lal Bairwa & Anr. vs. State of Rajasthan & Ors.,1
the present
                                                FIR cannot be quashed since it alleges offences inter-alia under
                                                section 6 of the PCOSO Act that are not private in nature. Mr. Vali
                                                further submits, that charges have been framed inter-alia under
section 174-A
IPC, which is a standalone offence against the court
                                                and cannot be quashed based on a settlement with the victim.

8.          The present case presents a peculiar set of circumstances : on the one
                                                hand there are allegation inter-alia under
section 6
of the POCSO Act
                                                and
section 174-A
IPC; but on the other hand, respondent No.3 has
                                                since married the petitioner; they have 02 children aged about 06
                                                years and 02 years; and respondent No. 3 has been cohabiting with the
                                                petitioner since 2018 itself. In fact the MoU recites that the petitioner
                                                and respondent No. 3 got married on 11.02.2018, i.e. 03 days after the
                                                subject FIR was registered.
9.          Learned counsel for the petitioner has also drawn attention to the
                                                statement of respondent No.3 recorded on 15.10.2019 under
section
                                                164
Cr.P.C., in which she has denied that she was under any coercion
                                                and has not even spelt-out anything to show that the offence was
                                                committed against her.
10.
In
Ramji Lal Bairwa
(supra), the Supreme Court has overturned the
                                                decision of the Rajasthan High Court, where the latter had quashed an
                                                FIR under the provisions of the
POCSO Act
in a case where the father
                                                of the minor victim had settled the dispute with the accused. In those
                                                circumstances the Supreme Court had observed, that proceedings in
                                                respect of heinous offences which are not private in nature and have
                                                serious impact on the society should not be quashed based on a
                                                compromise with the complainant.
11.         It must be mentioned here that
Ramji Lal Bairwa
(supra) concerned
                                                an assault by a teacher upon a minor student and the Supreme Court
                                                held that such offence cannot simply be described as an offence which
                                                is private in nature, with no serious impact on the society. It is in this
                                                backdrop that the Supreme Court observed as follows :

12.         However, as observed above, though in the present case there are
                                                allegations inter-alia under
section 6
of the POCSO Act, the current
                                                position is that the victim/respondent No.3 is now the wedded wife of
                                                the petitioner and has 02 minor children with him.
13.         Furthermore, since this court had a doubt whether proceedings under
section 174-A
IPC can be quashed merely on consent of the parties
                                                because that offence relates to disobedience of summons and warrants
                                                issued by a court, learned counsel for the petitioner explains, that the
                                                petitioner is a driver by profession and is ordinarily a resident of
                                                Mohali, Punjab; and had therefore not received the summons issued
                                                by the court in the matter, which was the reason for his non-
                                                appearance before the learned Trial Court.
14.         In the present case what prevails with the court is the incontrovertible
                                                fact that if the petitioner was to be prosecuted further in the matter,
                                                not only would the case end in acquittal since the principal witness
                                                against him would be his own wife (with whom he has 02 minor
                                                children and who has already been residing with him for more than 06
                                                years), the entire family including the 02 minor children would face
                                                serious hardship, leading to ruination of the entire family.

15.         In these circumstances, and in the larger interests of justice, and so as
                                                to not destroy a young family, this court is persuaded to accept the
                                                present petition.
16.         Therefore, in line with the spirit of the law
laid down by
the Supreme
                                                Court in
Gian Singh vs. State of Punjab & Anr.
reported as (2012)
                                                10 SCC 303 as also in
Narinder Singh & Ors. vs. State of Punjab &
                                                Anr.
reported as (2014) 6 SCC 466, this court sees no reason why the
                                                subject FIR and all proceedings emanating therefrom should not be
                                                quashed. This court is of the view that continuing with the subject FIR
                                                and all subsequent proceedings would be an exercise in futility and
                                                would not be conducive to peace and harmony between the parties.
17.         Accordingly, FIR No. 53/2018 dated 08.02.2018 registered under
section 363
IPC at P.S.: Kirti Nagar, Delhi is quashed. All
                                                proceedings arising therefrom also stand closed.
18.         Petition stands disposed-of.
19.         Pending applications, if any, also stand disposed-of.
